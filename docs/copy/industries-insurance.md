## 3. Who you actually sell to

**Section heading**
## Your buyer is not shopping for a policy

**Body**

The person on the other end of a commercial submission is a CFO, a controller, an operations
director, a risk manager or the owner of a 40-person contracting business. They are not comparing
premiums on a price table. They are trying to work out whether their current broker is any good, and
whether there is someone who understands their class of business better.

They come to that question at predictable moments. A renewal quote lands 30% higher than last year.
A carrier non-renews them. They win a contract that demands limits they do not carry. Their
experience mod moves. A claim goes badly. Someone on the executive team asks why the agency of
record has not been reviewed in nine years.

At that moment they do not call three brokers. They ask an assistant to explain what happened and
who handles this kind of risk. The shortlist forms there, before your producer has any idea the
account is in play.

**Pull quote**
The agency-of-record conversation now starts in a chat window.

## 4. What they research

**Section heading**
## The questions they type

**Intro**
These are the shapes of question a commercial buyer brings to an assistant. Some are coverage
questions, some are vendor questions, and the second kind is where you either appear or do not.

**Coverage and situation questions**
- "My workers comp premium went up 38% at renewal and my claims were flat. What happened?"
- "What is an experience mod and what actually lowers it?"
- "We won a municipal contract that requires $5M umbrella and a waiver of subrogation. What do I need?"
- "Our carrier non-renewed our habitational property. What are the options in Florida right now?"
- "Does a general contractor in Texas need builder's risk if the owner already carries it?"
- "What should a 60-person software company be paying for cyber liability?"
- "What has to be on a certificate of insurance before I let a subcontractor on site?"
- "Admitted versus non-admitted for a restaurant with a liquor licence. What is the trade-off?"

**Vendor questions**
- "Who are the best commercial insurance brokers for manufacturers in Ohio?"
- "Independent agent or captive agent for a multi-state contractor?"
- "Which brokers specialise in trucking fleets under 50 power units?"
- "How do I change my agent of record and what does that cost me?"
- "Which wholesale brokers write excess and surplus for habitational risk?"

**Body after the lists**
The coverage questions build the assistant's picture of the topic. The vendor questions decide who
gets named. Agencies that answer only the second kind never get retrieved for the first, and the
first is where the buyer starts.

## 5. The commercial problem

**Section heading**
## Referrals, renewals, and one channel

**Body**

Most independent agencies grow on two things: the renewal book and the referral network. Both work.
Neither is a channel you can turn up.

New business for commercial lines has historically arrived through centres of influence, bank and
accountant relationships, association memberships and producer networks. That pipeline is real, and
it is also capped by how many people your producers know.

Meanwhile the digital demand for commercial coverage has been consolidating into a small number of
platforms with very large content libraries. They publish thousands of pages on class codes,
coverage definitions and state requirements. Assistants retrieve from that kind of source because it
is comprehensive, structured and consistent.

An independent agency with 14 producers and a 30-page website is not competing with those platforms
on volume. It can compete on specificity, which is the one thing those platforms do not have. A
platform cannot tell a buyer which carriers currently have appetite for a 22-unit habitational
account in Broward County. A producer who writes that risk every month can.

The problem is that the producer's knowledge is not published anywhere a model can retrieve it.

## 6. How the four layers apply here

**Section heading**
## Four surfaces, applied to an agency

### SEO: Google search results

Coverage pages built by class of business and by state, not by product name. "Contractor insurance
in Ohio" is a buyer query. "Commercial general liability" is a glossary entry. Your carrier
appointments, your licensed states and your appetite are the facts that make these pages specific
instead of generic.

This is also the supply line. Assistants retrieve heavily from pages that already rank, so classic
search performance is an input to the AI layers, not a separate budget.
[More on SEO](/seo)

### Local SEO: Google Maps, the local pack, listings

For a multi-office agency this is a location problem and a category problem. A Business Profile
categorised as "Insurance agency" competes with every personal lines storefront in the county. The
commercial categories, the service list, the office-level detail and the hours are what separate a
commercial agency from an auto and home shop.

Reviews are the hard part in commercial lines. Businesses rarely review their broker, which means
review velocity is low and every review is worth chasing. We build the review request into the
renewal and claim-resolution moments, where the goodwill actually is.
[More on Local SEO](/local-seo)

### AI SEO: Google AI Overviews and AI Mode

Coverage-definition and requirement questions trigger AI Overviews constantly, because they have
clear factual answers. Google assembles those answers from passages it can extract and attribute.
That rewards pages where the answer is stated plainly near the top, with the state, the class and
the limit named, rather than buried under an introduction.

Since 31 August 2026, Search Console reports impressions from AI Overviews and AI Mode for every
site worldwide, so this layer is measurable from Google's own data.
[Source](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports) ·
[More on AI SEO](/ai-seo)

### GEO: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, Copilot

This is mostly work that does not happen on your website. Assistants assemble vendor answers from
sources they trust about you: association and Trusted Choice style directories, state licensing
records, trade press such as Insurance Journal and PropertyCasualty360, review platforms, local
business media, and community threads where people ask who to use.

Two things move it. First, entity clarity. Your agency name, address, licensed states, lines written
and named producers have to agree everywhere they appear, because a model that is uncertain who you
are will not recommend you. Second, third-party evidence. Commentary published under a named
producer's byline, in a place a model retrieves from, on a class of business that producer actually
writes.
[More on GEO](/geo)

## 7. What counts as a conversion here

**Section heading**
## Do not put a quote form on it

**Body**

A commercial submission needs an application, loss runs and usually a conversation. A "get a quote"
button collects personal lines traffic and wastes producer time. The conversion events that match a
commercial buyer's actual moment are different.

**What we set up instead**

| Conversion event | When the buyer is ready for it |
|---|---|
| Book a pre-renewal coverage review | 90 to 120 days before their renewal date |
| Request a certificate or contract review | They have won work with insurance requirements they may not meet |
| Talk to a producer about a specific class | They know their class and want someone who writes it |
| Experience mod or premium review | A renewal came back higher and they want it explained |
| Download a class-specific requirement checklist | Early research, not ready to talk |
| Direct call to the office | Always. Track it properly or you will under-credit this channel |

**Body after the table**
Renewal timing is the strongest signal an insurance agency has and almost nobody uses it. A buyer
asking about their renewal in October is in market for a 1 January effective date. That is a
qualification field, not a form filler.

Tracking matters as much as the form. Calls, form fills and booked reviews all have to land in one
place with the source attached, or the reporting will tell you this channel does nothing.

## 8. What delivery requires from you

**Section heading**
## This does not work without your producers

**Body**

The specificity that makes an agency retrievable comes from people who write the business. That has
a cost on your side, and you should know it before you sign anything.

**What we need**

- **Named producers willing to be published.** One hour a month each, from two or three people. A
  model retrieving commentary on contractor liability finds a person, not a logo.
- **Your appetite in writing.** Classes you want, classes you decline, states you are licensed in,
  minimum and maximum account size. This is the raw material for everything specific we publish.
- **Carrier relationships you are permitted to name.** Many carrier agreements restrict use of names
  and logos in marketing. We will not publish a carrier name until you confirm you may.
  `[NEEDS DATA: the client's carrier marketing permissions, per carrier.]`
- **A compliance path.** Insurance advertising is regulated at state level and published coverage
  commentary can create E&O exposure. We write to your reviewer and we do not publish coverage
  advice framed as a recommendation to a specific insured. Your compliance or E&O counsel signs off,
  not us. `[NEEDS DATA: named compliance reviewer and turnaround time, plus the states whose
  advertising rules apply.]`
- **Access.** Google Search Console, Google Analytics, your Google Business Profile, and the ability
  to publish website changes.
- **One decision maker.** Someone who can approve copy without a committee.

**What we never do**
Publish client names, loss data, premium figures or claim details. No named insured appears in
anything we produce without written permission from that insured.

## 9. FAQs

**Can you guarantee our agency shows up when someone asks ChatGPT for a broker?**
No. We do not control any of these systems, their retrieval changes without notice, and the same
question can return different answers on different days. We commit to the work, a prompt set you can
inspect, and a dated record of what we observed each time we ran it.

**We already have an SEO company. Is this the same thing?**
Ask them two questions: what is your AI Overviews impression trend in Search Console, and which
third-party sources currently cite us. If they do not report on either, they are working one of the
four surfaces. Classic SEO is inside our retainer because the AI layers depend on it, so this is
usually a replacement rather than an addition.

**Do you work with agencies that compete with each other?**
Not in the same class of business in the same state. We agree the exclusion in writing before the
engagement starts.

**Our producers are busy. How much of their time does this really take?**
About an hour a month each, from two or three of them, usually as a recorded conversation rather
than writing. We do the writing. We cannot do the knowing.

**How does this work if we have five offices?**
Each office is a separate local entity with its own Business Profile, listings and review programme,
sitting under one agency entity. The number of locations is the main thing that moves scope and
therefore price. [See how pricing works](/pricing).

**What if we are an MGA or wholesale broker and our buyer is a retail agent?**
Then your buyer is a producer at a retail agency asking an assistant which wholesaler writes a
class. The layers are the same, the prompt set is different, and the third-party sources that matter
are trade press and broker networks rather than consumer review platforms.
