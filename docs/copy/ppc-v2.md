## Addition 1. §5, who it suits

Current:

> Firms with an established sales process, a follow-up motion that works, and budget to run
> consistently for at least three months. Paid search does not fix a pipeline that leaks after the
> lead arrives.

**Add above that paragraph**, so the section opens on the ICP rather than on the caveat:

> B2B companies doing $3M to $1B+ in revenue, with 20 to 10,000+ staff. Wanting paid and nothing else
> is a complete reason to talk to us. You do not have to buy the search retainer, you do not have to
> care about AI search, and we will not spend the call trying to change that.

Then keep the existing paragraph unchanged, and keep the disqualifier paragraph unchanged. It matches
`CLAUDE.md` §2 exactly.

## Addition 2. §3, what we run

The six bullets stay. **Add one paragraph under them**, because "LinkedIn Ads for account and
job-title targeting where the buying committee is known" is doing a lot of unexplained work and the
buying committee is the thing that differs most between verticals:

> The buying committee is the targeting. In manufacturing it is a design engineer, then a procurement
> director, then a quality manager who can stop a deal after it is technically won. In software it is
> an economic buyer, a technical evaluator and a security reviewer who arrives late and kills
> bottom-up adoption. In professional services it is a managing partner and, separately, the partners
> who each own their own book. Naming those people and what each one disqualifies on is most of the
> work in a LinkedIn build, and it is why a campaign structure copied from another category burns
> budget.

Buying-committee roles are from `docs/research/b2b-vertical-substance.md` §3.1, §4.1 and §5.1.

## Addition 3. §6, add one FAQ

The four existing FAQs stay, including "Will paid help our AI search visibility? No." That answer is
the most valuable sentence on the page, because it is the claim every competitor makes and it is not
true.

**Add:**

> **Our industry is not one you have an industry page for. Does that matter for paid?**
> Less than it does for organic. What matters in paid is the query set, the negatives, the landing
> page and what happens after the form. We build the query set from your sales calls and your closed
> deals, not from an industry template. We will tell you on the call whether we have run paid in your
> category before, and where we have not, we will say so.
