## 3. Why a B2B firm should care about Maps

**Section heading**
## Two jobs, and most firms only know about the first

**The obvious job.** Buyers searching for a provider near them see the map pack before anything
else, and a firm that is not in the three results shown is not in the consideration set for that
search.

**The job that is newer.** Your Business Profile is one of the most structured, most trusted
descriptions of your firm on the internet. Google reads it. So does every system that scrapes or
licenses business data. When your Profile says one thing and your website says another, you have
created exactly the ambiguity that keeps a model from naming you.

---

## 4. What actually moves it for a B2B firm

**Categories.** The primary category does more work than anything else on the Profile. A commercial
insurance agency categorised as "Insurance agency" is competing with every personal lines storefront
in the county. Pick the category that describes the business you want, not the business you have
listed.

**Office-level truth.** Each office is its own entity, with its own Profile, its own address, its own
named manager, its own hours and its own service list. Shared assets across five offices produce five
weak entities.

**Review velocity, which is the hard part.** B2B clients do not review their broker, their accountant
or their MSP unprompted. Review counts in these categories are low, which means every review moves
the needle further than it would in a consumer category. The fix is asking at the right moment: after
a renewal, after a resolved claim, after a filing deadline is met, after an incident is contained.

**Listing consistency.** Name, address, phone and description identical across the directories that
matter in your category. Not most of them. All of them.

**Named people.** Profiles, bios and public presence for the individuals buyers actually work with.
This is what connects the local entity to the expert content that drives [GEO](/geo).

## 5. The work

- Business Profile claim, categorisation, completion and ongoing management, per office
- Listing audit and correction across category-relevant directories and data aggregators
- Review programme built into your existing client moments, with a request process someone owns
- Review response, including the negative ones, in your voice
- Local landing pages for offices that genuinely serve a distinct market, and no others
- Local entity reconciliation against your site, your schema and your listings
- Reporting on Profile views, direction requests, calls and the conversions your CRM recorded

## 6. What we will not do

Create Business Profiles for addresses that are not real offices. Build a location page for every
city within an hour's drive. Write or incentivise fake reviews. Countries and regions you serve are
never presented as offices you have.

## 7. FAQs

**We serve clients nationally. Does Maps matter?**
It matters for the entity even when it does not drive volume. A complete, consistent, correctly
categorised Profile is one of the sources that confirms who you are to every system that reads it.

**We have seven offices. Is that seven times the work?**
Roughly, yes, and it is the main thing that moves scope and price. [See pricing](/pricing).

**How do we get reviews from business clients?**
By asking at the moment something went well, from the person they actually deal with, with a link
that takes two taps. We build the process. Your team runs it, because it has to come from them.
