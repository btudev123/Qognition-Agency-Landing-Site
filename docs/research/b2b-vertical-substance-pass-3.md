### 2.3 Comparison axes this vertical actually shortlists on

**Inferred** from 2.1 and 2.2, and stated as inference.

1. **Approval scope.** Not "are you certified" but "is this aircraft, this engine family, this process, this task on your rating and capability list". Binary, first, and most suppliers fail here silently.
2. **Slot or turnaround, with a date.** Hangar slot availability, shop turnaround days, AOG response hours. Availability outranks price here for the same reason pass 1 found it outranks technical performance in industrial components.
3. **Traceability and documentation quality.** For 16.04 this is the entire deal. For 16.02 it is the shop visit report and the back-to-birth pack.
4. **Downtime cost, not invoice cost.** For 16.03 an extra week on the ground can outweigh the quote difference. Almost nobody publishes a downtime estimate.
5. **Safety audit standing.** For 16.07 and 16.08, the third-party rating is the shortlist filter before anyone talks price.
6. **Who else flies this.** Reference operators on the same type. The most requested and least published item on any MRO site.

**Inferred, and it is the aerospace content gap:** axis 1 stated at task level. A page titled "what our Part 145 certificate actually covers, task by task, aircraft by aircraft" is a page no MRO publishes and every DOM wants. It is structured, factual and self-contained, which makes it retrievable as well as useful. Same logic as pass 1's AVL-justification artefact.

### 3.3 Comparison axes this vertical actually shortlists on

**Inferred.**

1. **Can you be sourced at all.** IATF 16949 certificate with the right scope, capacity at the program volume, financial health, and a second source. Binary.
2. **Can you launch.** PPAP history, run-at-rate evidence, launch-support engineering. The axis that separates surviving suppliers from quoted ones.
3. **Coverage and availability.** In aftermarket and equipment, catalogue coverage percentage, fill rate and delivery window. This is axis one for 17.04, 17.06, 17.13 and 17.14.
4. **Service network geography.** For 17.12, 17.13 and 17.14, the map decides it. Publishing a real coverage map with response times beats every adjective.
5. **Total cost including downtime and residual.** For fleet and equipment, the purchase price is the smallest term.
6. **Integration and data terms.** Specific to 17.07 and the single most decisive axis there.

**Inferred, and it is the automotive content gap:** axis 6. Every dealer-software vendor publishes features. None publishes what the DMS integration will cost the dealer, who charges it, and what the switching path looks like. That is the exact analogue of pass 2's finding that vertical SaaS never publishes the cost of leaving, and here the fee is charged by a third party, which makes it easier to write about honestly.

### 4.3 Comparison axes this vertical actually shortlists on

**Inferred.**

1. **Can you carry the risk.** Insurance limits, indemnity language, EMR, prequalification platform status. Procurement checks this first and last.
2. **Coverage against my site list.** Not "we serve the Midwest" but "we self-perform at these 43 addresses and subcontract at these 7". Almost nobody publishes the split.
3. **Staffing model and retention.** In janitorial, guarding and grounds, turnover is the service. Buyers ask about it and providers answer with adjectives.
4. **Compliance evidence per visit.** Inspection reports, deficiency tracking, proof of service. The deliverable is the product in 18.05, 18.10 and 18.11.
5. **Price transparency against the incumbent invoice.** In route-based and waste, the comparison is literally line-by-line against the current bill.
6. **Escalation and response time, with a number and a measurement method.**

**Inferred, and it is the facility-services content gap:** axis 1. A page titled "our insurance limits, our EMR, our prequalification status and what your procurement team will ask us for" would be the only one of its kind in the category and would be forwarded internally by the champion, which is the same mechanism pass 1 identified for the AVL justification artefact in manufacturing.

### 4.5 Conversion event, by sub-industry

### 5.3 Comparison axes this vertical actually shortlists on

**Inferred.**

## 7. Franchising & Multi-Location (21) · 17 sub-industries, 113 niches, 45 priority-A

### 7.1 The error that makes franchising pages wrong, stated first

Positioning rule 2 says the buyer is always the B2B firm: "Home services franchisors is the franchisor recruiting franchisees, never a homeowner." That rule is necessary and not sufficient. The sufficient version:

**A franchisor runs two marketing functions with two different buyers, two different budgets and two different owners.**

| Function | Buyer | Budget | Internal owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Franchise development** | the prospective franchisee, an investor or career changer | franchisor P&L | Chief Development Officer, VP Franchise Development |
| **Brand fund and local marketing** | the franchise's end consumer or business customer | the brand fund, collected from franchisees | CMO, with the **Franchise Advisory Council** watching |

Industries 21.01 to 21.10 are franchisors. **The ICP is the franchise development function.** Everything written for them must be about recruiting franchisees. The moment a page drifts into "we will drive leads for your locations" it has crossed into the brand-fund function, where the end buyer is frequently a consumer, and rules 2 and 4 are both in play.

21.13 and 21.17 straddle both, which is why they are the two most delicate builds in the industry.

**Inferred**, and it is the most likely way this industry gets written wrong.

### 7.4 Comparison axes this vertical actually shortlists on

Two different sets, because there are two buyers.

**A candidate comparing franchise concepts (21.01 to 21.10):**
1. **Total investment and liquidity requirement**, against what they actually have.
2. **Item 19**, and whether there is one at all. A franchisor without an Item 19 is at a structural disadvantage and every candidate guide says so.
3. **Territory availability** in their market. Binary, and the most common silent disqualification.
4. **Validation**: what existing franchisees say when called.
5. **Ramp time to breakeven**, and whether it is disclosed or implied.
6. **Semi-absentee feasibility**, which is now the dominant framing in the investor segment.

**A franchisor comparing suppliers (21.11 to 21.17):**
1. **Franchise-system experience, by system size band.** A 40-unit emerging brand and a 900-unit system are different businesses.
2. **Compliance literacy.** Whether the supplier understands FDD constraints, registration states and Item 19 limits without being told.
3. **Cost per qualified candidate measured to Discovery Day or signing**, not to lead.
4. **Franchisee adoption**, for anything that touches the franchisees.
5. **FAC and franchisee-association acceptability.**

**Inferred, and it is the franchising content gap:** axis 2 on the supplier side. A supplier page that demonstrates command of the disclosure constraints, and states what it will and will not say on a franchisor's behalf because of Item 19 and the July 2024 FTC guidance, would be the only one of its kind and would be forwarded straight to franchise counsel, which is the person who kills these deals.
