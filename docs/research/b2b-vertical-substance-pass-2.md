### 2.3 Comparison axes this vertical actually shortlists on

1. **Does it fit the stack I already have.** Named integrations, not "integrates with your tools."
2. **Time to first value,** stated in days, with what the customer must do.
3. **Total cost including the internal headcount to operate it.** The build-versus-buy argument lives here.
4. **Will it survive our security review.** For dev tools this is the whole risk. Trust centre, subprocessor list, SOC report type and date, penetration test recency.
5. **Switching and migration cost out**, which in vertical SaaS is the deciding axis and is almost never published.
6. **Roadmap and changelog velocity.** For dev tools, a stale changelog is a disqualifier.

**Inferred, and it is the SaaS content gap:** axis 5. Almost every SaaS vendor publishes migration *in*. Publishing what leaving costs is the single most credibility-generating page a vertical SaaS vendor can write, and nobody writes it.

### 3.2 The constraint that governs everything client-facing, and it is the ABA Rule 7.1 analogue

Pass 1 found that lawyer advertising is rule-governed and that Qognition's house rule on unmeasured numbers makes it structurally easier for a law firm to buy from. **Financial services has the same structure, and it is even sharper, because it has two separate regimes.**

**SEC Marketing Rule, Advisers Act Rule 206(4)-1** (adopted 22 Dec 2020, **effective 4 May 2021**, compliance date November 2022). It is unlawful for an investment adviser to engage in any fraudulent, deceptive or manipulative act in an advertisement. Testimonials and endorsements are permitted **only if** the adviser satisfies disclosure, oversight and disqualification provisions: advertisements must **clearly and prominently disclose whether the promoter is a client and whether the promoter is compensated**; Rule 206(4)-1(b)(3) prohibits compensating a promoter subject to a disqualifying event where the adviser knows or should know; and the adviser must have a **written agreement with promoters**, except affiliates and de minimis compensation of **$1,000 or less**. Performance claims require retained documentation showing the basis for or calculation of the performance, under amended **Rule 204-2**, kept **at least five years after the adviser stops using it**.
([sec.gov Investment Adviser Marketing](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/investment-adviser-marketing), [sec.gov Marketing Compliance FAQs](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/division-investment-management-frequently-asked-questions/marketing-compliance-frequently-asked-questions) · **reported**, via SEC's own domain through search results)

**FINRA Rule 2210** for broker-dealers. Communications are classified as correspondence, retail communications or institutional communications. All communications must be **based on principles of fair dealing and good faith, fair and balanced, provide a sound basis for evaluating the facts, and include all material facts or qualifications** necessary so they are not misleading. **All retail communications must be approved by an appropriately qualified registered principal before the earlier of use or filing.** Certain retail communications must be filed with FINRA's Advertising Regulation Department (10 business days prior for a new member's first year; within 10 business days of first use for registered investment company, DPP and CMO material). Records must include the dates of first and last use, the approving principal and the date of approval, **and information concerning the source of any statistical table, chart, graph or other illustration used.**
([finra.org Rule 2210](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2210), [What and When to File](https://www.finra.org/about/how-we-operate/advertising-regulation/what-when-to-file) · **reported**)

**This is the most commercially useful paragraph in this file, and I want to be explicit about why.** FINRA 2210's recordkeeping rule requires a firm to be able to name the source of any statistic it publishes. `CLAUDE.md` §4 already forbids Qognition from publishing an unsourced number. **An agency whose house rule is already "sourced and dated or it does not ship" is the only kind of agency a compliance officer can approve without rewriting the engagement.** That is demonstrable rather than asserted, it is a differentiator no competitor in §1 has, and it is the same argument pass 1 made for law firms. It generalises: legal, financial services, and to a lesser degree healthcare all buy the discipline before they buy the channel.

**Corollary, and it is a hard constraint on our own copy:** any Qognition case study naming a wealth-management or broker-dealer client, or quoting one, may itself be a testimonial or endorsement under 206(4)-1 or a retail communication under 2210 **for the client**, not for us. `[NEEDS DATA: legal review of whether a Qognition-published client quote from an RIA triggers the client's own 206(4)-1 disclosure and written-agreement obligations. This is OPERATOR-DECISIONS shaped and it must be answered before any financial-services proof block ships.]`

## 5. Construction, Real Estate and Built Environment (tier B, 8 sub-niches)

**Pass 1 flagged this as the loosest vocabulary of the set and the highest risk of a confident error. That assessment was correct and I am treating it accordingly. This section has more `[NEEDS DATA]` than any other, deliberately.**

### 5.1 The error that makes construction pages wrong, stated first

Construction is not one vertical, it is **three markets with different buyers who share a vocabulary**:

1. **Contractors and trades selling to owners** — bid-driven, prequalification-gated, no demo exists.
2. **Product and material suppliers selling to contractors and specifiers** — spec-driven, closest in shape to manufacturing (pass 1 §3).
3. **Software selling to any of the above** — SaaS shaped, but with a field-adoption gate no other SaaS vertical has.

The taxonomy mixes all three inside `construction-real-estate`. **A single page template across these will be wrong for at least two of them.** Specifically: `commercial-contractors` and `modular-prefab` are market 1; `building-materials` and `hvac-electrical-roofing-distribution` are market 2 and should inherit the manufacturing/distribution treatment from pass 1 §3.3; `proptech` and `construction-tech` are market 3 and should inherit §2 of this file; `aec-firms` and `cre-services` are professional services and should inherit pass 1 §5.

**Second error, and it is the one I would most expect a writer to make:** conflating **residential contracting** with **commercial construction**. The competitor listicles in this vertical are overwhelmingly about *residential remodeling, roofing and home services* (Siana's list is literally "custom remodelers"; Bluelinks' "contractors" list names Contractor Gorilla, Lemon Seed Marketing, Footbridge Media, all home-services shops). `commercial-contractors` in the taxonomy says "Commercial contractors, GCs and specialty trades." **These are different buyers with different conversion events, and the AI-search supply side is crowded in residential and thin in commercial.** That distinction is the actual opportunity here and every competitor list I opened blurs it.

### 13.2 The trigger-reversal pattern, and it is the most commercially useful thing in this file

| Vertical | What reversed | Date |
|---|---|---|
| Agriculture / food / logistics | FSMA 204 compliance moved from 20 Jan 2026 to **20 July 2028** | Congressional directive, 2026 |
