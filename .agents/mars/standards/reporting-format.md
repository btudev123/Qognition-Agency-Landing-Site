# Reporting format

Applies to anything a client reads: monthly reports, QBR decks, audit documents, proposals.

## Shape

**One page the client reads in ninety seconds, then an appendix.** If the summary needs a second page, the summary is wrong, not the page limit.

The one-pager carries: the conversion event and its number, the comparison, what was done, what goes wrong next, and the one decision needed from the client. Nothing else. Charts, tables, keyword lists, campaign breakdowns and screenshots go in the appendix.

## Rules

1. **Lead with the client's conversion event.** Not traffic. Not impressions. The thing named as the primary conversion event in `context.md`. Traffic appears only as an explanation of why that number moved.

2. **Compare against three things:** the previous period, the same period a year ago (or the earliest available, stated as such), and the agreed target. A number with no comparison is decoration.

3. **Never present a metric without saying what was done to move it.** "Organic sessions up 22%" is not a report. "Organic sessions up 22% after the September comparison-page cluster went live" is.

4. **If something went backwards, it goes at the top.** Not buried in the appendix, not softened, not sandwiched between two wins. Say what happened, why, what has already changed, and when you will know whether the fix worked.

5. **Attribution honesty.** If the platform-reported number and the client's own revenue data disagree, show both and say which one you trust and why. Never quietly pick the flattering one.

6. **No metric without provenance.** Every figure names its source and the date it was pulled. A figure with neither is `[NEEDS DATA]`.

## Banned in client reporting

- Percentages without absolute numbers. "Conversions up 300%" on a base of two is a lie by formatting.
- Cherry-picked date ranges chosen to make a line go up.
- Screenshots of dashboards in place of an interpretation.
- "Continue to monitor" as a next action. Monitoring is not an action.
- Any forecast presented as a projection rather than an assumption, with its inputs unnamed.
